Sign Language Interpreter — v1

6th sem · minor project
Detection-based gesture recognition, published as an IEEE-format conference paper

Hearing loss affects roughly 430 million people worldwide, and interpreters aren't always accessible or affordable. This project set out to translate hand gestures into text in real time, using nothing but a webcam — no wearables, no depth sensors. A four-person team built a custom dataset for six core gestures (Hello, I Love You, No, Yes, Thank You, Please), hand-annotated it in LabelImg, and trained a YOLOv5 object-detection model to recognize gestures directly as bounding boxes in the video feed.

Sign Language Interpreter — v2

final year project · upgrade
Landmark-based recognition with a desktop GUI, built as the direct upgrade of v1

v2 rebuilt the same problem from a different angle: instead of detecting bounding boxes per frame (heavier, and capped at the six gestures it was trained on), it uses MediaPipe to extract 21 hand-landmark coordinates per frame, normalizes them for position invariance, and classifies the resulting feature vector with a Random Forest model — chosen over CNNs and SVMs for its balance of accuracy, speed, and small-dataset efficiency. The vocabulary grew to the full alphabet, digits 0–9, and space (37 classes, 3,600 images), and the whole thing runs inside a PyQt5 desktop app with a live gesture overlay, a running sentence buffer, and clear/reset/save controls — turning single-letter predictions into readable words in real time.
